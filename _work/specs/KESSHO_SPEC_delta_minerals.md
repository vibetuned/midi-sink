## §4 Minerals and crystal habits

### 4.1 The split

A mineral has two halves and they live in two shaders:

* **Optical (composite.glsl, pure reader):** how light reads the field. Switching minerals leaves the field bitwise (the §10.1 gate extends to every mineral).
* **Mechanical (deform.glsl, exact operators):** how the crystal *admits* a strike and a stroke — the drop's **habit** R(θ) and the tine's **cleavage profile** F(y). These change the field and carry §10.2 gates.

The two halves are independent parameters: a Calcite optic over a Barite rosette is allowed; the presets (§4.6) pair them the way nature does.

### 4.3 The symplectic crystal-drop theorem (ratified)

In action-angle coordinates about the centre, ρ = r²/2 and θ (dx∧dy = dρ∧dθ), the v2 drop of radius r₀ is the translation ρ_src = ρ − r₀²/2. Replace r₀ by any profile R(θ) > 0:

$$\rho_{\text{src}} = \rho - \tfrac{1}{2}R(\theta)^2, \qquad \theta_{\text{src}} = \theta$$

This is a kick generated by V(θ) = R(θ)²/2 — the "kick depends on the angle only" case of §2.1 — so

$$J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -R R' \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det J = 1 \ \text{exactly, for any } R(\theta), \text{ any amplitude, no sub-stepping.}$$

Four properties the first draft did not state:

1. **Bijective for any R(θ) > 0.** Each ray maps onto itself monotonically (r_src = √(r² − R²) for r ≥ R). "Star-shaped about the centre" is the only condition and it is automatic.
2. **Boundary growth is free.** Scaling R → sR is the same map with V = (s² − 1)R²/2, so `press_mode` 0 grows a rhomb exactly; the area is ∝ s², so s = √(1 + p) for "area linear in pressure".
3. **The interior coordinate is r/R(θ).** The ink phase inside a crystal drop is assigned from r/R(θ), not r, so the rings are homothetic copies of the habit (rhombs inside a rhomb). With r the rings would be circles inside a rhomb.
4. **Corners crease.** Where R′ jumps, J jumps: the bands outside an unregularised polygon show a crease along each vertex ray. In Kessho that crease is a birefringence line and reads as cleavage. `habit_roundness` regularises it; zero is allowed.

**Habit profiles** (cartesian in the drop's own frame, rotated by the strike's angle):

* `RHOMB` — the calcite cleavage rhomb, face angles 78.1° / 101.9°: the polar support form R(θ) = min_i d_i / cos(θ − φ_i) over the faces with cos > 0; roundness as a log-sum-exp soft-min with radius k(roundness) `[ITERATE]` by eye.
* `HEX` — the regular hexagon (muscovite's pseudo-hexagonal sheet), same construction.
* `ROSETTE` — m tabular blades, R(θ) = r₀·(1 + β cos^{2p}(mθ − θ₀)), p sets blade sharpness, m = `habit_blades`. **Formula corrected:** the first draft's Σ_j cos^{2p}(m(θ − θ_j)) puts m lobes on each of m blades.

### 4.4 Cleavage as tine profiles (ratified with a fix)

A shear x_src = x − F(y) in the stroke's frame is exact for any F(y) (§2.1). The first draft's profiles are exact but **monotone** — the twin ramp displaces the two half-planes against each other (a fault), the staircase is a global shear plus a wiggle — and either pulls texels in from outside the canvas, failing zero fabrication at the first soak. The fix keeps exactness and costs nothing: **F(y) is a bump, zero outside |y| < H**, i.e. a tine profile.

* `TWIN_KINK` (Baumhauer): F(y) = γ·pwl(y) — a piecewise-linear bump (up at slope γ over the lamella, flat, down), the shear concentrated in two kink lines where J jumps and Kessho draws two sharp lamella edges.
* `TERRACE` (phyllosilicate slip): F(y) = A·stair_N(y)·w(y), stair_N(y) = ky − sin(2πNky)/(2πN) (monotone, N flat terraces), w a C² window to zero at ±H.
* `SMOOTH` is Jaffer's α/(α + d): bitwise the v2 tine.

The profile is a property of the tine, so `bend_mode` 0 (glide) and the ripple inherit it; no new operator.

### 4.5 Optical laws by optic class

Common to all classes: retardation Γ_eff = Γσ + Γ₀ → the Michel-Lévy LUT; Layer B as §1.3.

* **Isotropic (Fluorite):** Γ = 0. Layer A is dark; the medium shows Layer B alone. This is a diagnostic view as much as a mineral.
* **Uniaxial (Quartz, Calcite, Selenite):** the §1.2 brush sin²(2(ψ − α)). **Walk-off** (Calcite): the ink/aux layers are sampled twice, at x and at x + w, **w = δ·sin(2(ψ − α))·ê_ψ** (corrected from the first draft's sin 2ψ: the split collapses when the strain axis meets the crystal axis and cross-fades with the brush), blended cos²(ψ−α) : sin²(ψ−α). The caustic is not doubled.
* **Biaxial (Muscovite, Barite):** the brush is replaced by the two-axis product |sin(ψ − α + V)·sin(ψ − α − V)|. Around a vortex ψ sweeps once per turn while σ decays radially, so the isochromatic contours become two-lobed — lemniscate-shaped. **Stated as a stylisation** of the conoscopic figure, not Biot–Fresnel (that law lives in angle space, which a texel does not have). **Thin film** (Muscovite): the ink channel ∈ [1, 3) read as sheet thickness h through Airy's R(Λ) = 4r² sin²(δ/2) / ((1 − r²)² + 4r² sin²(δ/2)), δ = 4π n h cos θ_s / Λ_RGB, with cos θ_s from σ (sheet tilt); three evaluations per texel.
* **Dispersion** (Barite; any mineral may set it): three caustic depths z_R < z_G < z_B — three offset iso-trace folds tr J = 2 + 1/(z(z−1)), a rainbow on the fold for two extra scalar evaluations.
* **Conical ring** (Barite): where |Δ| < ε, the pre-image is sampled on a small ring (N = 8 points, radius `conical_ring`) and averaged — Hamilton's hollow cone on the fold. Masked tight; the one moderately expensive law.
* **Sensitive tint** (Selenite): Γ₀ = 550 nm puts the unstrained field at first-order magenta; the sign of a shear strain shifts it toward blue or yellow. **The sign of strain becomes visible** — the plain chart cannot show it.

### 4.7 Deferred

**Suzu conical intersection** (`Mineral.md` §2.C.3): a 2×2 coupled-mode cell with (p, t) → ω± = ω₀ ± √((κ₁p)² + (κ₂t)²), the Berry π flip around the diabolical point. Correct as maths; **parametric-drive class** (the coupling is time-varying), and the sign flip is inaudible without a second voice to interfere with. The double-cone drawing in the trace is the only payoff. → Phase 13, `[ITERATE]`, DECISIONS_10.
