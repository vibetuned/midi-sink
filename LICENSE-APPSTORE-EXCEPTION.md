## Why this exists

Curated application stores — Apple's App Store above all — distribute only
binaries they have signed, under store terms that restrict what a recipient
may do with their copy: how many devices it may be installed on, technical
measures that prevent installing a modified build, and a prohibition on
redistributing the store's binary. Those are "further restrictions" of the
kind AGPL-3.0 section 10 forbids, and section 6 requires Installation
Information that a store-signed build cannot meaningfully provide.

Without an explicit exception the author could not lawfully publish midi-sink
on such a store. Granting it costs users nothing: the complete source of every
release stays published under the AGPL, and anyone may build, modify and
redistribute it with no store involved.

curated distribution platform (each, a "Store"), and in doing so:

1. to accept and comply with the Store's terms of distribution and its

   recipient that sections 4, 5, 6, 7 or 10 of the AGPL would otherwise
   forbid — including limits on the number of devices, technical measures
   that restrict the installation of modified versions, and restrictions on
   further redistribution of the Store's copy; and

2. to convey that object code without the Installation Information that
   section 6 would otherwise require, where the Store's signing regime would
   render such information ineffective.

This permission applies **only** to copies conveyed through a Store. It
attaches to no other copy of the Program.
