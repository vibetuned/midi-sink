Suzu lab

The synth inside Voxo, played in your browser by the engine itself: every panel's sound and every panel's drawing come from the same voice, state and all.

The pages hold no physics. Voxo's Suzu, the C++ the desktop plays, is compiled to WebAssembly and runs in the browser's audio thread; the page sends it the MIDI a controller would, takes its sound, and reads each voice's state about sixty times a second: the orbit of a cell, the energy in each mode, the shape of a string, the pressure along a bore, the opening of a reed.

How faithful is it? The web build and a native build of the same source render one script and are compared sample by sample, state by state. Most voices agree to the last bit. Where they do not, the two platforms' math libraries round a sine, a tanh or a power differently in the last place, and the difference stays more than 80 decibels under the sound.

Each page has a lab mode with red controls: the update that blows up, the junction that leaks, the step that breaks the stability bound. They are the reasons the engine's gates exist. The lab mutes the sound and restarts the engine when one of them runs away; nothing a page offers reaches the speakers at full scale.

The sound starts on each page with its button: a browser plays nothing before a click.
