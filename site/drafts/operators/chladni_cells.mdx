Sprinkle sand on a plate and bow its edge: the sand gathers on the nodal
lines of the plate's standing wave. midi-sink cannot gather ink — every
operator here preserves area exactly, and Liouville's theorem says an
area-preserving map never changes density — but it can **stretch**, and the
Chladni operator stretches the ink along the edges of the keys.

**The cells are the eddies.** The layout's keys are circles — the ones the
tablets draw — and every one of them is an eddy. A texel inside a key turns
about the key's centre by

$$
\theta \,\big(4\rho^2(1-\rho^2)\big)^2,\qquad \rho = r/R,
$$

zero at the centre, full at $\rho = 1/\sqrt 2$, zero again at the rim with
zero slope, so the note's drop rests where it fell and is wound from its
edge while the water between the keys never moves. A rotation preserves
$r$, so the map has $\det J = 1$ inside every key and is continuous across
the touching rims: **class exact**, and the negative pass is the exact
inverse. Layouts that draw no keys — the circle of fifths, the rolls — take
the largest circle at each note that touches no neighbour's.

**Two senses.** Neighbouring keys counter-rotate on the layout's own
checkerboard; the balance control turns the odd keys' sense from opposite
through still to the same.

**The inverse Chladni.** A second mode sums every key's ring into one
divergence-free flow instead of turning each key alone. The keys may then
grow past their circles and overlap, and the water between them is stirred
by both neighbours — the burst's class, area-preserving to first order, a
pass at a time.

## Invariants

* **Exact.** Measured on the desktop harness after 150 stirred frames: the
  key centres and the corners between keys move less than a quarter texel
  while the rings move sixteen; a ring round a centre turns 1.45 rad and
  stretches 0.09, a ring round a corner does nothing.
* **The eddies are the keys.** The stir's discs are the layout probe's
  cells, same centres and radius — the geometry the tablets draw.
* **It bakes, and only bakes.** The flow writes into the field while the
  stir is held, a pass whenever it carries two quanta of displacement,
  never an absolute. The dip is the undo.
* **The ingress rule** applies where a key is clipped by the canvas edge.

## Ownership

* **`SUMI_CTL_CHLADNI_A`** (the stirring rate) and **`_B`** (the odd keys'
  sense), unmapped in the core, CC 106 and 107 on the desktop;
  `chladni_cell`, the eddy's disc as a fraction of the key (capped at the
  key in the exact mode); `chladni_mode`, exact discs or the inverse
  Chladni. Under the Anod medium's binding table poly pressure drives the
  stir.
* **`sumi_add_chladni(psi, balance, s_x, x_0, s_y, y_0)`** as a gesture: one
  step of the two-wave cellular flow on a lattice of its own; a negative
  `psi` applies the exact inverse.

## Lineage

Ernst Chladni's figures (1787) are the picture; the exact rotation inside a
disc is Rankine's vortex with a smooth profile, the operator the vortex
family already used; and Aref and Ottino's chaotic advection is why
iterating it winds the ink. The two-wave gesture is Taylor and Green's
cellular vortex (1937) split into two exact shears.
