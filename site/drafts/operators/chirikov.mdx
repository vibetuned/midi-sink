The most famous chaotic map there is, on water. One step of the **Chirikov
standard map** kicks every column by a sine of its position and then drifts
every row by its distance from the centre line:

$$
y_1 = y + A\sin\!\big(k(x - x_c) + \varphi\big),\qquad x_1 = x + \varepsilon\,(y_1 - y_c).
$$

Each is a shear, so each is exact: area is conserved to the last texel at
any strength, and the step's inverse is the drift undone first, then the
kick. In the map's own variables $X = kx$, $Y = k\varepsilon y$ this is
$X' = X + Y'$, $Y' = Y + K\sin X$ with one number in charge:

$$
K = A\,k\,\varepsilon .
$$

**Greene's threshold.** Below $K_c \approx 0.9716$ the map's invariant curves
survive: iterate the step and the ink slides along smooth sheets, wrapping
the island that sits half a kick period from the centre. Above it the last
invariant curve breaks and a chaotic sea opens; ink placed in it filaments
exponentially, while the island — smaller now, ringed by chains of smaller
islands — still holds its rings. The textbook map lives on a torus; the
sheet does not wrap, so the drift is scaled by $\varepsilon$ and every
rotating orbit eventually marches off the side. What stays on the sheet is
what librates.

**Played by throws.** The map takes the mod wheel (or breath) as
*deltas*, never a position: a change $\delta$ of the control applies one
step with the kick and the drift both scaled by $\delta$, so the step's
chaos parameter is $\delta^2 K_{\max}$. Ease the wheel over many frames
and you play the pendulum flow — smooth, integrable, sheets; throw it in one
frame and you play one hard kick — chaos. Move it back and the inverse
steps undo the way up. Each step's $K$ is capped in the core at the erosion
ceiling the gate set, because chaotic stretching is exactly what a resampled
sheet erodes fastest.

## Invariants

* **Exact.** One step at $K = 0.5$ moves the central band's pre-image
  thirty-three texels on the desktop harness; the step then its inverse
  leaves four hundredths of a texel — smooth shears, the resampler's floor.
  The pass matches the closed form to a tenth of a texel.
* **The transition, on a sheet.** After sixty steps, rings on the
  hyperbolic point keep about half their ink at $K = 0.5$ (the librating
  half stays, the rotating half streams off) and a sixth at $K = 1.5$ (the
  chaotic sea flushes the inside too); rings on the island keep all of it.
* **The ceiling.** The route's per-step $K$ is capped in the core at the
  value the erosion table set; the gesture's at 2, where the medium stops
  rendering the map at all.
* **The ingress rule** applies to both stages, as to the ripple.

## Lineage

Boris Chirikov's standard map (1969, 1979) is the universal model of the
transition to Hamiltonian chaos; John Greene's residue method (1979) put
the threshold at 0.971635. Here the map is the kick-drift pair of the
Chladni and spark shears turned to a single sinusoidal kick and a linear
drift — the same symplectic splitting, the same exact inverse by reversed
order — and the erosion soak of step 35 is the gate that tells this
instrument how deep into chaos its medium can follow.
