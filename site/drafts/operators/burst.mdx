> Method after A. Jaffer, "The Lamb–Oseen Vortex and Paint Marbling"
> (arXiv:1810.04646), extended here to the multipoles m ≥ 2.

Strike the water with an impulse that has no net push and no net spin —
a **quadrupole** — and four lobes form: two eject along an axis, two draw
in across it. In a viscous sheet the impulse's vorticity diffuses as it
acts, and the flow it drives dies on its own clock. The burst is the whole
of that motion, integrated over time into one displacement, the way Jaffer
integrated the decaying Lamb–Oseen vortex into a single closed-form turn.

**The integral closes.** The $m$-th viscous multipole has the stream
function $\psi_m \propto \gamma_m(s)\,\sin(m\theta)/r^m$ with the cutoff
$\gamma_m(s) = 1 - e^{-s}\sum_{k<m} s^k/k!$ and $s = r^2/4\nu\tau$ — the
Lamb–Oseen vortex is $m = 0$, the viscous stroke's Stokeslet $m = 1$.
Integrating over the life of the flow,

$$
\int_0^t \gamma_m\!\left(\tfrac{r^2}{4\nu\tau}\right) d\tau
  = \frac{r^2}{4\nu}\,\Phi_m(S),\qquad
\Phi_m(S) = \int_S^\infty \frac{\gamma_m(s)}{s^2}\,ds,\qquad S = \frac{r^2}{4\nu t},
$$

and by parts $\Phi_m = \gamma_m(S)/S + \Gamma(m-1, S)/(m-1)!$. For the
dipole the remainder is the exponential integral $E_1$ — the logarithmic
kernel that makes the stroke special. For every $m \ge 2$ it is an
exponential polynomial, and $\Phi_m$ is elementary; for the quadrupole,
$\Phi_2 = \chi(S) = (1 - e^{-S})/S$, the stroke's own $\chi$. With the
impulse spread over a Gaussian core of radius $a$ and aged to $\ell$
($\ell^2 = a^2 + 4\nu t$),

$$
\Psi = A_m \left(\tfrac{a}{r}\right)^{m-2} \sin\!\big(m(\theta-\theta_0)\big)
       \left[\Phi_m\!\left(\tfrac{r^2}{\ell^2}\right) - \Phi_m\!\left(\tfrac{r^2}{a^2}\right)\right],
\qquad \mathbf d = \nabla^{\perp}\Psi .
$$

**Where the pinch comes from.** Near the core the quadrupole's displacement
is pure hyperbolic strain, $d_x = \lambda x,\ d_y = -\lambda y$: the pinch
operator is this burst's $r \to 0$ limit, as the Lamb–Oseen swirl's core is
solid-body rotation. In the diffused zone $a \ll r \ll \ell$ the quadrupole
decays as $\cos 2\theta / r$ (order $m$ as $\cos m\theta / r^{m-1}$); beyond
$\ell$ every order falls as $1/r^{m+1}$, the potential multipole the
vorticity has not yet reached. The burst is to the pinch what Lamb–Oseen is
to solid-body rotation: the physical bridge between the core and the far
field.

**The strike has a lifetime.** It fires sharp at $\ell = a$ and the release
grows the age to its final value over the life; $\ell^2$ grows linearly in
time, so most of the motion lands early and the tail is soft — the
discharge blooms and dies. The amplitude $D$ is the displacement of the lobe
at $r = a$ over the whole burst; the age shapes the strike without scaling
it. $\theta_0$ aligns the ejection lobes — the pen's azimuth, or the glide
direction, once the medium's binding tables route the strike.

## Invariants

* **Sub-stepped.** $\mathbf d$ is divergence-free as a field; the finite map
  preserves area to first order. The core splits every burst into passes
  whose peak displacement stays within a fraction $\beta_m$ of the pass's
  current core — the wake's $a/4$ criterion ($|\nabla \mathbf d| \le 0.25$)
  measured for each order — and one such pass reads a pre-image Jacobian
  of at least 0.81 everywhere with mean 1.00000 on the desktop harness. A
  burst and its negative leave the first-order residual, a fraction of a
  texel at the core.
* **The stagnation origin.** The centre does not move; the lobes at $r = a$
  move by exactly $D$, outward on the axis, inward across it.
* **The shader is the closed form.** Along the axis the desktop harness reads
  the displacement at $a/2$, $a$, $2a$, $3a$, $4a$ to a fortieth of a texel
  of the double-precision reference.
* **The age envelope.** At a sixth of the release the lobe has moved the
  share $[\Phi_2(1/3.5) - \Phi_2(1)]/[\Phi_2(1/16) - \Phi_2(1)] = 0.70$ of $D$
  (age 4); at the end, $D$, less the medium's half-float quantum.
* **The ingress rule** (fresh water where a pass reaches beyond the canvas)
  applies as to the wake.

## Ownership

* **`sumi_add_burst(x, y, a, D, θ₀, m)`** — the gesture, and the strike
  itself: it starts the episode whose increments the engine emits over the
  release. `burst_age` (the final diffusion length over the core, 1.5–12)
  and `burst_life` (the release in seconds; 0 fires the whole burst at
  once). A negative $D$ is the first-order inverse. The order clamps to
  2–8; the note-on strike route and the pitch-class → $m$ table arrive with
  the medium's binding tables.

## A note on where this formula came from

I wasn't looking for a theorem — I was looking for a spark. The strikes in
the electric medium felt too round, too liquid, nothing like the discharge
I see when the music peaks. So I did what this whole project does: I
followed Professor Jaffer's method. His Lamb–Oseen paper integrates a
viscous flow over time to get a closed-form displacement; I tried the same
integration on the higher multipoles, expecting special functions — and for
m ≥ 2 the integral simply closed. The logarithmic kernel that haunts the
dipole vanishes, and everything becomes elementary. I searched the
literature as well as I could and did not find it stated in this form, but I
claim nothing: serendipity did the work, and Jaffer drew the map. I only
needed the discharge to bloom sharp and die soft, the way it sounds in my
head. Here is its closed form.

— *the author*

## Lineage

The viscous multipoles — the Stokes-approximation solutions of the vorticity
diffusion equation for a localized impulse, the Hermite modes of a
diffusing vortex — are classical fluid mechanics (Voropayev & Afanasyev;
Chan & Chwang's unsteady singularities; Gallay & Wayne). Aubrey Jaffer's
integration of the decaying Lamb–Oseen vortex into one closed-form
displacement (2018) is the method, applied here to the orders above the
dipole. The wake's viscous stroke is the $m = 1$ case, with its $E_1$; the
pinch is the quadrupole's core; the algebraic lobe the early roadmap
sketched is superseded by the burst and never shipped.
