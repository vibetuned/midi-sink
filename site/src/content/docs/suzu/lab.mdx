Every Suzu chapter has a live panel, and the panel is not a drawing of the
synth: it is the synth. Voxo's Suzu, the same C++ the desktop plays, is
compiled to WebAssembly and runs in your browser's audio thread. The page
sends it the MIDI a controller would, receives its sound, and reads the
state of every voice about sixty times a second: the pressure along a bore,
the shape of a string, the opening of a reed, the energy in each mode. What
you see is what you hear, drawn from the same numbers. The sound starts on
each page with its button: a browser plays nothing before a click.

The lab lives beside the marble app, at <LabLink>**/marble/suzu/**</LabLink>
(the release candidate's under `/marble/rc/` while one is newer), composed
from the same release tag — it is engine code, and the
documentation site contains no second implementation of any voice, as it
contains none of any operator.

Each page has a lab mode with **red controls**: the update that blows up,
the junction that leaks, the step that breaks the stability bound. They
are the reason the engine is built the way it is, and the worklet mutes
full scale and restarts the engine when one runs away.

## How faithful is it

The web build and a native build of the same source — the same flat
surface, without fused multiply-add — render one script and are compared
sample by sample, state by state. For most voices they agree to the last
bit: the cell, Verlet, the hybrid, Duffing, the rotor and the sax. Where
they differ — the lattice, the flute, the trumpet — it is because the two
platforms' math libraries round a sine or a power differently in the last
place, and the difference stays more than 80 decibels under the sound. The
negative control is red. The chaotic voices are the exception that proves
the rule: a last-bit difference grows, as chaos does, and only their
statistics can be compared.

The page gates run in headless Chrome: every page's own check (the drift,
the bow's convergence, the overblow at breath 116 of 127, the ledger's
inequality) through `?gate=`, and the captures the evidence keeps.

## The lab's MIDI

A page plays from its own keys and sliders; the flute page also listens to
Web MIDI where the browser has it, so a wind controller's breath blows the
bore in the browser as it does on the desktop. The lab is deviceless — Voxo
without an output device, the worklet pulling its blocks — so there is no
latency to tune and nothing to install.
