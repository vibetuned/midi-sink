## The magic circle

One cell is one rotation in phase space, and its orbit is the circle you
see. Per sample,

$$
x \mathrel{-}= \varepsilon\,y,\qquad y \mathrel{+}= \varepsilon\,x \ \text{(the updated } x\text{)},
\qquad \varepsilon = 2\sin\!\left(\pi f / f_s\right).
$$

This is the Gordon–Smith oscillator (1985; the "magic circle" of the
synthesis folklore). The two half-steps are each a shear — $\det = 1$ each,
$\det = 1$ together — and the quantity $x^2 + y^2 - \varepsilon x y$ is
conserved exactly, so the orbit keeps its size for ever: the oscillator is **exact in pitch** (within
0.000 cent across MIDI 21–108 at 44.1 k and 48 k, because $\varepsilon$ is
the exact rotation's coefficient, not a Taylor series) and **incapable of
drift**. A strike sets the orbit — $x = A$, $y = \varepsilon A / 2$, the
amplitude the velocity's (0.25 at 127) — and the release is a declared
contraction per sub-step, a T60 the patch names. The output is $x$ through
an attack ramp (a mixer stage, not a map on the cell) and the press's
level.

**The MPE correction.** A glide retunes $\varepsilon$ every frame; a
retune alone would change the orbit's size (the conserved quantity has
$\varepsilon$ in it), so the cell is **re-based** onto the new $\varepsilon$
with its amplitude kept: ±48 semitones over two seconds costs 0.097 dB
against the naive form's 0.63 for a one-block jump.

## The shears

A shear of one coordinate by a function of the other is still symplectic:
$x \mathrel{-}= \varepsilon\,(y + s(y))$ with a cubic $s$ bends the circle
and the bend is the harmonics the spectrum shows arriving. A symplectic
cubic is a gentle waveshaper — the third harmonic 33 dB under the
fundamental at a shear of 0.3, 23 dB at full gain — and it detunes the
cell; the cell calibrates the shear's detune at start-up and compensates it,
so every note stays within a fifth of a cent at full gain (the calibration
within 0.19 cent for the cubic, 0.02 for the fold). The two shear kinds are
the *cubic* and the *fold*; the hard timbres belong to the
[Duffing cell and the kicked rotor](../strings-and-chaos/). The shears run
in a 2× section; the fold's sharper corners would want a 4× budget with a
half-band filter, an open item carried rather than spent.

## The filter

The state-variable filter is the thematic form — the Chamberlin core at
twice the rate, $q = 2 - 1.9\cdot$*resonance* — a **declared dissipation**:
its $q$ is the one place in the cell where $\det < 1$, and it is the same
everywhere. CC 74 scales the cutoff by $2^{(t - 0.5)\cdot 6}$, three octaves
either way about the patch's. The exact tuning of the Chamberlin core holds
for the undamped ring only; with damping the ring sits 2.3–2.5 cents sharp
at C8 at $Q = 50$, so the coefficient is solved per block for the *damped*
ringing frequency in double precision — the ring within 0.008 cent across
the keyboard.

## Denormals, and the rest

Sixty-four released voices' tails stay flat to the end: this Apple CPU
shows no denormal cliff with flush-to-zero off, and the x86 boxes have
theirs to show. Sixteen cells with the filter and a shear take 3 % of the
audio callback (0.09 ms worst of 2.67 at 128 frames and 48 k).
