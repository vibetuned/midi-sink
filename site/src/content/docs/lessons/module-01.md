## 1.1 Historical prologue: from crystal optics to symplectic geometry

Symplectic geometry was born in 1832 not in mechanics but in crystal optics,
when William Rowan Hamilton used his characteristic function to predict
**conical refraction** in biaxial crystals. midi-sink keeps the lineage in two
places. The **crystal drop** of the [Kessho](../../operators/minerals/) medium
(`SUMI_MEDIUM_KESSHO`, libsumi 1.6.0; the minerals 1.7.0) is written in
Hamilton's action-angle coordinates, where Jaffer's expansion becomes a
translation and a crystal habit becomes a kick generated by a function of the
angle alone (§1.4, Family 3). And the Barite mineral carries Hamilton's hollow
cone as a stylisation: where the trace discriminant is near zero and the sheet
strains, the caustic's light is read through a ring kernel of eight points
(`conical_ring`, §10.7.8) — a line on the fold doubling into two. The engine
does not solve Fresnel's wave surface; it reads a strained sheet between
crossed polarizers.

Whether we track light through strained calcite, pigment rings floating on
water (Sumi, the suminagashi medium), or the phase-space trajectory of an
oscillator (Suzu, the synth inside Voxo), the governing structure is the same
**closed, non-degenerate differential 2-form** $\omega$, and the one rule that
is never broken is the canvas's own: no element may create area it was not
handed ([the DSP class table](../../suzu/)).

## 1.2 Differential forms and symplectomorphisms in 2D and 4D

Let $M = \mathbb{R}^{2n}$ (or a cotangent bundle $T^*Q$) be phase space with
canonical coordinates $(q_1, \dots, q_n, p_1, \dots, p_n)$.

1. **The tautological 1-form.** On $T^*Q$ the canonical 1-form is
   $\theta = \sum_i p_i\, dq_i$. Integrating $\theta$ around a closed loop
   $\gamma = \partial\Sigma$ measures the action $I = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_\gamma p\, dq$.

2. **The symplectic 2-form.** Its exterior derivative is the canonical 2-form

   $$\omega = -d\theta = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dq_i \wedge dp_i .$$

   Because $d^2 = 0$, $\omega$ is **exact** on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ and therefore
   **closed**: $d\omega = 0$. (On a general symplectic manifold only closedness
   is required; exactness is the special gift of $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ and of
   cotangent bundles, and it is what lets §1.4 write every exact operator from
   a generating function.)

3. **Symplectomorphisms and $\det J = 1$.** A smooth map $\Phi: M \to M$ is a
   symplectomorphism if it preserves the 2-form under pullback,
   $\Phi^*\omega = \omega$. In 2D ($n = 1$: the libsumi canvas $(x, y)$, or a
   single Suzu cell $(x, y)$), $\omega = dx \wedge dy$ is the oriented area
   form. For $\Phi(x, y) = (u(x, y), v(x, y))$ with Jacobian

   $$J = D\Phi = \begin{pmatrix} u_x & u_y \\ v_x & v_y \end{pmatrix},$$

   the pullback is evaluated directly by the wedge product:

   $$\Phi^*(dx \wedge dy) = du \wedge dv = (u_x v_y - u_y v_x)\, dx \wedge dy = (\det J)\, dx \wedge dy .$$

   Thus on a 2D manifold $\Phi$ **is symplectic if and only if $\det J = 1$
   everywhere.** This is the sentence the whole engine is built on: the
   operator book's *exact* class is "det J = 1 at any magnitude"
   (§10.2), and Suzu's *symplectic* row is "det of the per-sample map = 1
   exactly" (§13.1).

## 1.3 Why Eulerian grids fail and why libsumi stores displacements

Advect pigment on a grid with an Eulerian velocity solver and the bilinear
interpolation of pigment density introduces numerical diffusion that blurs
sharp suminagashi rings into mud within seconds (§4, the founding rule: no
Navier–Stokes *solvers*; the Lamb–Oseen swirl is Navier–Stokes entering as an
exact *solution*, §4.3(7)).

libsumi instead models deformation as a **group composition of closed-form
inverse-lookup symplectomorphisms** on two ping-pong textures, `tex_A` and
`tex_B`, **RGBA16F by default** (RGBA32F behind a desktop quality flag; §4.1).
For every output texel $P$ the engine asks where that water came from,
$P_{src} = \Phi^{-1}(P)$, and reads the ink there.

Rather than storing the raw pre-image coordinates $(u, v)$ — where half-float
precision degrades to $2^{-11}$ across the outer half of the canvas — each
texel stores, since libsumi 1.2.0 (Phase 8 step 55b, `DECISIONS_7 #1`), the
**accumulated displacement from the identity**:

$$d(P) = P_{pre}(P) - P = (u - x,\; v - y), \qquad \text{payload } (u - x,\; v - y,\; \text{ink},\; \text{aux}).$$

At rest $d(P) = (0, 0)$, so the half-float quantum follows the displacement:
$2^{-24}$ canvas heights at rest, $2^{-15}$ at a sixteenth of the canvas. When a
new pass with inverse map $\Phi^{-1}(P) = P_{src}$ is applied, the shader
re-bases the lookup (§4.2):

$$d'(P) = d(P_{src}) + (P_{src} - P).$$

Because the Jacobian of a composition is the product of Jacobians,
$J(\Phi_2 \circ \Phi_1) = (J\Phi_2)(J\Phi_1)$, composing any number of
$\det J = 1$ passes preserves $\det J = 1$ to floating-point rounding — and the
regression check that proves the composition is exact is that forty passes of
a $z/40$ tine equal one pass of a $z$ tine (§4.6).

## 1.4 The exactness rule (§10.2 additions, KESSHO §2.1)

In interactive graphics we want deformations that are **spatially localized**
— decaying away from a note's cell. But multiply a symplectic kick or shear by
a naive radial window $W(r)$ and you immediately break $\det J = 1$.

### Proposition 1.1 (the windowed-kick defect)

Take action-angle polar coordinates $(\rho, \theta)$ about a drop centre, with
$\rho = \tfrac12 r^2$ so that $dx \wedge dy = r\, dr \wedge d\theta = d\rho \wedge d\theta$
is canonical. Apply a radially windowed action kick without touching the angle:

$$\rho_1 = \rho - W(\rho)\, V'(\theta), \qquad \theta_1 = \theta .$$

Then

$$
J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - W'(\rho) V'(\theta) & -W(\rho) V''(\theta) \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \det J = 1 - W'(\rho)\, V'(\theta) \neq 1 .
$$

Any spatial window on the coordinate being kicked breaks area conservation by
(window slope) × (kick). This is not a hypothetical: it is the desktop bench's
**red control**. `midi-sink --dev --kessho-flat` evaluates the first draft's
windowed Arnol'd kick on the CPU beside the shipped operators and reads
$|\det J - 1|$ of 50 at the maximum and 0.35 rms, as required
(`DECISIONS_10 #4`).

From this the spec states the rule every new operator is written under
(§10.2 additions):

> **Exact + localised ⟹ twist-type, or generating-function (implicit, Newton
> in the shader), or a kick generated by a function of the angle alone.**
> Everything else is sub-stepped and must say so.

These notes sort the shipped operators into four constructions. The spec's
three families are Families 2, 4b and 3 below; Family 1 is the "shear that
ignores its own coordinate" the spec's first sentence names, and Family 4a is
the pinch's own trick, exact but not compactly supported.

### Family 1 — pure coordinate shears (tines, cleavage profiles, the sine ripple, the chord wave)

In a local frame $(x, y)$ aligned with a stroke direction $\hat D$, let

$$x_{src} = x - F(y), \qquad y_{src} = y .$$

Because $F$ depends only on $y$, the Jacobian is upper-triangular with ones on
the diagonal:

$$J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -F'(y) \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \det J = 1 \quad \forall F .$$

This holds for every tine profile the engine ships (`tine_profile`, §10.7.8,
`deform.glsl`'s `tine_fs`), with $d = |y|$ the perpendicular distance,
$H = 4\alpha$ and $u = d/H$:

| profile | $F$ | where $J$ jumps |
|---|---|---|
| `SUMI_TINE_SMOOTH` (0) — Jaffer | $z\,\alpha/(\alpha + d)$, bitwise the v2 tine | nowhere |
| `SUMI_TINE_TWIN_KINK` (1) — Baumhauer's lamella | $1$ for $u < 1/3$, then $1.5(1 - u)$ to zero at $u = 1$ | two kink lines, which Kessho draws as the lamella's edges |
| `SUMI_TINE_TERRACE` (2) — phyllosilicate slip | $v - \sin(2\pi N v)/(2\pi N)$, $v = 1 - u$ clamped, $N = 4$ flat terraces | the terrace risers |

Every profile is a **bump, zero beyond $H$**. The first draft's monotone ramp
and staircase were exact too, but a monotone shear pulls texels in from outside
the canvas and fabricates ink — the soak measured −99.9 % of the mass in 500
"pairs" (an odd profile reversed is the same shear, so $\pm z$ is not a pair).
The bench keeps that fault as profile 3, dev only, as `--tine-fault`'s red
control (`DECISIONS_10 #6`). The sine ripple $A\sin(ky + \phi)$ (§4.3(6)) and
the chord wave — a ripple-tine windowed *across* its band, i.e. by the
coordinate the shear ignores (§10.8.2) — are the same family.

### Family 2 — twist maps in action-angle coordinates (vortices, Lamb–Oseen, torsion, Chladni)

In $(\rho = r^2/2, \theta)$, rotate the angle by a function of $\rho$ alone:

$$
\rho_{src} = \rho, \qquad \theta_{src} = \theta - \Theta(\rho)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad J_{(\rho,\theta)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -\Theta'(\rho) & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \det J = 1 .
$$

Twist maps are the one explicit family that is both exact and compactly
supported in $r$ (§10.2 additions). That is why, as shipped in `vortex_fs`,
`swirl_fs` and `cells_fs`, none of these needs sub-stepping at any amplitude
and every $\pm A$ pair inverts analytically:

* the exponential vortex $\Theta(r) = A\, e^{-r/R}$ and the Rankine vortex
  (rigid for $r < R$, $\omega R^2/r^2$ outside — all the shear in the crease
  ring at $R$) — §4.3(3);
* the Lamb–Oseen swirl $\Theta(r) = \dfrac{S}{2\pi r^2}\left(1 - e^{-r^2/r_c^2}\right)$,
  $S = \Gamma\,\Delta t$ — §4.3(7), the viscous rotation, $C^\infty$;
* the wave torsion $\Theta(r) = A\sin(kr - \phi)\, e^{-r/R}$ — §10.3, rings of
  alternating angular shear, an outward wave when $\phi$ sweeps;
* the Chladni eddy, the exact rotation of one disc of radius $R$ by
  $\Theta = \theta_0\, w\, \big(4 s^2 (1 - s^2)\big)^2$ with $s = r/R$ — the ring
  peaks at $s = 1/\sqrt2$ and vanishes at the centre and the rim (`cells_fs`,
  mode 0; its mode 1, the summed "inverse Chladni" field, is the sub-stepped
  class).

### Family 3 — angle-generated kicks: the symplectic crystal-drop theorem (§10.7.8)

Exchange the roles of $\rho$ and $\theta$ and kick $\rho$ by a potential
$V(\theta) = \tfrac12 R(\theta)^2$ that depends on the angle alone:

$$\rho_{src} = \rho - \tfrac12 R(\theta)^2, \qquad \theta_{src} = \theta \qquad (r \ge R(\theta)).$$

In the Cartesian radius $r = \sqrt{2\rho}$ this maps each ray $\theta$
monotonically onto itself, $r_{src} = \sqrt{r^2 - R(\theta)^2}$. Its Jacobian
in $(\rho, \theta)$ is

$$
J_{(\rho,\theta)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -R(\theta) R'(\theta) \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \det J = 1 \text{ identically.}
$$

For $R \equiv r_0$ this is Jaffer's circular drop, the translation
$\rho_{src} = \rho - r_0^2/2$; `drop_habit` 0 is bitwise the v2 drop.

**Consequences, as ratified in §10.7.8 and built in `drop_fs`:** (1) a drop need
not be circular to be exact — any star-shaped habit $R(\theta) > 0$ conserves
area identically, at any amplitude, with no sub-stepping; (2) boundary growth
is free — scaling $R \to sR$ is the same map; (3) the interior ink phase is
assigned from $r / R(\theta)$, so the rings inside are homothetic copies of
the habit; (4) **corners crease** — where $R'$ jumps, $J$ jumps across the
vertex ray, and the bands outside crease along it; in Kessho that crease is a
birefringence line and reads as cleavage. The shipped habits, in the drop's own
frame (the strike's angle), softened by a log-sum-exp with
$k = 2 + 40(1 - \text{roundness})^2$ and then mixed toward the circle by
`habit_roundness`:

| `drop_habit` | $R(\theta)$ | paired by the presets with |
|---|---|---|
| `SUMI_HABIT_RHOMB` (1) — the calcite cleavage rhomb, faces 78.1° / 101.9° | $r_0 / \max\big(\lvert\sin\theta\rvert, \lvert\sin(\theta - A)\rvert\big)$, $A = 78.1°$, $r_0$ the inradius | Calcite, Selenite (gypsum's cleavage plate) |
| `SUMI_HABIT_HEX` (2) — the regular hexagon | $r_0 / \max_i \lvert\cos(\theta - 60°\, i)\rvert$ | Quartz, Muscovite's pseudo-hexagonal sheet, Fluorite (the cube on its diagonal) |
| `SUMI_HABIT_ROSETTE` (3) — $m$ tabular blades, `habit_blades` | $r_0\,(1 + \beta\cos^{8}(m\theta)) / (1 + \beta\cdot 35/128)$, $\beta = 0.6$, the mean radius $r_0$ | Barite, $m = 5$ |

The pairing is the shells' and the six `presets/kessho_<mineral>.json`; the
optical half and the mechanical half are independent parameters, and a
Calcite optic over a Barite rosette is allowed.

### Family 4 — invariant-streamline windows and generating functions

**4a. The Hamiltonian pinch (§4.3(5)).** The global hyperbolic saddle
$x_{src} = x e^{k}$, $y_{src} = y e^{-k}$ is generated by $H(x, y) = xy$.
Because $s = xy$ is conserved along every trajectory, windowing the exponent by
$w(s) = e^{-|s|/S}$ keeps the map closed-form:

$$x_{src} = x\, e^{+k\, w(xy)}, \qquad y_{src} = y\, e^{-k\, w(xy)},$$

and with $w' = dw/ds$,

$$\det J = \big(1 + k s w'\big)\big(1 - k s w'\big) - \big(k x^2 w'\big)\big(-k y^2 w'\big) = 1 - k^2 s^2 w'^2 + k^2 s^2 w'^2 = 1 .$$

The caveat the spec states as a feature: the window cannot decay along the two
fold axes themselves ($s = 0$ there), so the pinch's arms run outward as fading
creases — which is what pinched paper does. The window is in $s$, not in $r$,
so the pinch is exact but not compactly supported: it is not a counterexample
to Family 2's uniqueness.

**4b. Type-2 generating functions — the Arnol'd tongue lock (`SUMI_DEFORM_ARNOLD`, §10.8.1).**
When a kick *must* be radially windowed by $W(\rho_1)$, derive the map from a
mixed-variable generating function
$F_2(\theta, \rho_1) = \theta\rho_1 + \frac{K}{q} W(\rho_1)\cos q(\theta - \varphi)$:

$$
\rho = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial \theta} = \rho_1 - K\, W(\rho_1) \sin q(\theta - \varphi), \qquad
\theta_1 = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial \rho_1} = \theta + \frac{K}{q}\, W'(\rho_1) \cos q(\theta - \varphi).
$$

The radial slope $W'$ is compensated by an angular twist, and $\det J = 1$
exactly because the map is canonical by construction. As shipped in
`arnold_fs`: one iteration $M = T \circ \text{Kick}$ about a voice's drop, the
twist $T$ a Lamb–Oseen-shaped rotation with $\Theta(R) = 2\pi\Omega_0$ turns,
$C^2$-windowed to zero by $2.5R$; $W = 1$ on $[\rho_{drop}/2, \rho_{drop}]$ with
quintic ramps to zero at the centre (a bijection needs $W(0) = 0$) and at
$1.5\rho_{drop}$; the lookup is $M^{-1}$ — the twist undone, then the kick's
inverse by **one scalar Newton iteration on $\theta$, five steps**, monotone
while $|K W'| < 1$, hence $K \le K_{max} = 0.8 / \max|W'|$. It is Kessho's
press default (`press_mode` 3): the tempered fifth's two-cent beat precesses
the islands.

## 1.5 Reading the Jacobian with shaders: Sumi, Anod and Kessho (§10.7.1–§10.7.4)

From the stored displacement $d(x, y) = (u - x, v - y)$ the composite shader
reconstructs the inverse-lookup Jacobian $J = I + \nabla d$ by finite
differences:

$$J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + \partial_x d_x & \partial_y d_x \\ \partial_x d_y & 1 + \partial_y d_y \end{pmatrix}.$$

Two stencils, as shipped in `composite.glsl`: the short one (Anod's, one-sided
minimum, the half-float rounding bias subtracted) gives $\psi$ and $\sigma$; the
trace for Layer B is read from a wide window (±3 texels at 512), because the
fold at a large $z$ sits a few hundredths above $\operatorname{tr} J = 2$ and a
texel-wide difference would read the quantum.

Take the polar decomposition $J = R_\phi\, U(\psi, \lambda)$, with $R_\phi$ a
rotation and $U$ symmetric positive-definite, principal stretches
$\lambda \ge 1 \ge \lambda^{-1}$ along the axis $\psi$. $J$ is the inverse map's
Jacobian, so $C = J^T J$ is the forward flow's *left* Cauchy–Green tensor —
Eulerian, which is what a polarizer on the screen sees (§10.7.1). Because
$\det J = 1$, every spectral invariant reduces to the Frobenius norm
$\lVert J\rVert_F^2$ and the trace $\operatorname{tr} J$:

1. **Principal strain and the birefringence retardation (Anod, Kessho Layer A).**
   Since $\lambda^2 + \lambda^{-2} = \operatorname{tr}(J^T J) = \lVert J\rVert_F^2$,
   $(\lambda - \lambda^{-1})^2 = \lVert J\rVert_F^2 - 2$, so the scalar shear
   strain is

   $$\sigma = \lambda - \lambda^{-1} = \sqrt{\lVert J\rVert_F^2 - 2},$$

   and the principal axis is

   $$\psi = \tfrac12 \operatorname{atan2}\big(2 C_{12},\; C_{11} - C_{22}\big), \qquad C = J^T J .$$

   Since 3.0 Layer A reads $\sigma$ through the **strain buffer** — averaged
   over the afterglow's seconds (§10.10) — so the fringes build and settle
   instead of snapping. Layer A is then the mineral's optical law (§10.7.8):

   $$I_A = I_{\text{backlight}} \cdot \text{body} \cdot \text{brush}(\psi, \alpha) \cdot \mathrm{ML}(\Gamma\sigma),$$

   with the brush by optic class — **isotropic**: dark (Fluorite shows Layer B
   alone); **uniaxial**: $\sin^2 2(\psi - \alpha)$, the isoclinic brush that
   paints the dark **Maltese cross** in a vortex's shear annulus (Quartz,
   Calcite, Selenite); **biaxial**: the cross splits into two brushes $2V$
   apart, $\lvert\cos^2 2V - \cos^2 2(\psi - \alpha)\rvert / \max(\cos^2 2V, \sin^2 2V)$
   — a stylisation of the conoscopic figure, since that law lives in angle
   space, which a texel does not have (Muscovite $2V = 40°$, Barite $37°$).
   $\Gamma$ is the mineral's retardation at unit strain scaled by the *Exposure*
   knob (`kessho_retard`/2000 — the ratios between minerals are physical,
   $\Delta n$), and $\mathrm{ML}$ is the Michel–Lévy chart as a 1024-texel LUT the
   engine builds from a spectral integration against the CIE 1931 curves
   (`core/src/kessho_lut.h`, `tools/kessho_lut.py`; beyond 2200 nm the orders
   wash to high-order white). The sensitive-tint plate (Selenite,
   $\Gamma_0 = 550$ nm) replaces the brush by $\Gamma_{\text{eff}} = \lvert\Gamma_0 + \Gamma\sigma\sin 2\psi\rvert$:
   the *sign* of the strain becomes visible. The three-channel
   $\sin^2(\pi\Gamma\sigma/\Lambda_{RGB})$ form is the fallback for the web demo
   only (KESSHO §1.2) — it is what the standalone lab below uses.

2. **The trace discriminant: the elliptic/hyperbolic partition (Kessho Layer B).**
   The eigenvalues of $J \in SL(2, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy
   $\mu^2 - (\operatorname{tr} J)\mu + 1 = 0$, with discriminant
   $\Delta = (\operatorname{tr} J)^2 - 4$:
   * **elliptic**, $\Delta < 0 \iff \lvert\operatorname{tr} J\rvert < 2$: purely
     rotational motion ($\mu = e^{\pm i\phi}$; vortex cores, Chladni eddies) —
     the cool tint;
   * **hyperbolic**, $\Delta > 0$: exponential stretching along unstable
     manifolds (saddles, wakes, Chirikov chaos) — the warm tint;
   * **parabolic**, $\Delta = 0$: the order-to-chaos boundary.

   The tint's weight is $\text{smoothstep}(0.05, 0.8, \lvert\Delta\rvert)$ — the
   staircase's noise sits under 0.05. The honest headline (§10.7.1): rigid
   rotation has $C = I$, so $\sigma = 0$ and $\psi$ is undefined — Layer A is
   dark exactly where Anod is dark, and $\operatorname{tr} J = 2\cos\phi$ is the
   *only* local observable that detects rigid elliptic rotation. What Kessho
   adds over Anod is orientation ($\psi$) and rotation ($\operatorname{tr} J$).

3. **The collapsed Lagrangian caustic and the $z = 1$ flatness gate (§10.7.3).**
   Refract rays through the displacement gradient $\nabla d = J - I$ onto a bed
   at depth $z$; the light focuses inversely to the ray-map determinant. For any
   $2\times2$ matrix, expanding $\det((1 - z)I + zJ)$ gives

   $$\det(I + z\nabla d) = (1 - z)^2 + z(1 - z)\operatorname{tr} J + z^2 \det J .$$

   * **When $\det J = 1$:** $(1 - z)^2 + z^2 = 1 - 2z(1 - z)$, and the
     determinant collapses to Kessho's one line, exactly as `kessho_caustic_w`
     evaluates it:

     $$\det(I + z\nabla d) = 1 + z(1 - z)\,(\operatorname{tr} J - 2).$$

     The fold ($\det = 0$) is an **iso-trace contour, not the parabolic
     horizon**: for $z > 1$ it sits on $\operatorname{tr} J = 2 + \dfrac{1}{z(z - 1)}$
     and sweeps from $+\infty$ toward $2^+$ as $z \to \infty$, so the depth
     control (`caustic_z`, 1.05..8, log-spaced) scans a level set of
     $\operatorname{tr} J$ through the hyperbolic regime toward the parabolic
     horizon. The shader draws it as a line (by `fwidth`) over a soft glow, and
     Barite's dispersion reads it at three depths $z/\sqrt{1 + d},\, z,\, z\sqrt{1 + d}$
     — a rainbow on the fold.
   * **When $z = 1$:** the uncollapsed determinant is $\det J$ itself.
     Evaluating it at $z = 1$ is therefore a direct detector of $d\omega \ne 0$:
     any exact operator renders flat, and a windowed kick lights up. The depth
     1 is reachable only through the dev flag, and `--kessho-flat <operator|all>`
     is the gate — **as measured** (`DECISIONS_10 #4`), not as proposed: the
     identity sheet reads 0 exactly; an exact Lamb–Oseen twist on the CPU reads
     $1.4\cdot10^{-3}$ at the maximum (the stencil's truncation); the GPU's
     three-drop scene reads a median of $2\cdot10^{-3}$ (±3 texels) to
     $5\cdot10^{-3}$ (±1), with a p99 of 0.2–0.8 and a maximum of 8–30 owned by
     the drops' rims, where the inverse of an exact expansion has an unbounded
     radial derivative — the pre-image's one singularity, exact and
     unresolvable by any stencil, so the rims are masked. A few thousandths is
     the field's floor; the proposal's $10^{-4}$ cannot be held by a half-float
     field, and the class stays *declared*, with the gate as its runtime check.

## 1.6 Phase space in audio: Suzu's magic circle, retuning and twisted 2-forms (§13)

Now drop from the 512 × 512 canvas to a single oscillator voice in Suzu
(`voxo/src/suzu.h`, `struct Cell`). Where the canvas says *area-preserving*,
the synth says *amplitude-stable*.

### 1. The symplectic magic circle versus the naive update (§13.2)

A harmonic oscillator of frequency $f$ at sample rate $f_s$ has the step
parameter $\varepsilon = 2\sin(\pi f / f_s)$ — the prewarp, so the discrete
rotation angle is exactly $2\pi f/f_s$.

**The naive simultaneous update** (`update_mode` 1, the lab's red control;
`Cell::step_naive`, the suite's negative control):

$$
x_{n+1} = x_n - \varepsilon y_n, \quad y_{n+1} = y_n + \varepsilon x_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\varepsilon \\ \varepsilon & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \det J = 1 + \varepsilon^2 > 1 .
$$

Phase-space area grows by $1 + \varepsilon^2$ every sample. The suite's drift
gate at 440 Hz, $A = 0.5$, 48 kHz: over the 0.1 dB bound at 1.0 s and
**+690.2 dB after 600 s**.

**The symplectic leapfrog, the magic circle** (`update_mode` 0, `Cell::step`):
using the already-updated $x_{n+1}$ in the $y$ update composes two exact
shears:

$$x_{n+1} = x_n - \varepsilon y_n, \qquad y_{n+1} = y_n + \varepsilon x_{n+1} = \varepsilon x_n + (1 - \varepsilon^2) y_n ,$$

$$
J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ \varepsilon & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\varepsilon \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\varepsilon \\ \varepsilon & 1 - \varepsilon^2 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \det J = 1 \text{ identically.}
$$

Moreover $\operatorname{tr} J = 2 - \varepsilon^2 = 2 - 4\sin^2(\pi f/f_s) = 2\cos(2\pi f/f_s) \in (-2, 2)$
for all $f < f_s/2$, so $\Delta < 0$: the oscillator is strictly **elliptic**
— §1.5's partition, one octave down — with the exact rotation angle
$\phi = 2\pi f/f_s$. The same gate: ten minutes undriven and undamped,
amplitude +0.0001 dB, frequency +0.000 cent; the tuning within 0.000 cent
across MIDI 21–108 at 44.1 and 48 kHz.

### 2. The skewed invariant and MPE retuning (`Cell::retune`)

$\det J = 1$ guarantees bounded orbits, but the magic circle's level curves are
not circles $x^2 + y^2 = A^2$; they are the $\varepsilon$-sheared ellipses

$$I_\varepsilon(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - \varepsilon x y = A^2\Big(1 - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\Big),$$

on which $x$ peaks at $A$ where $y = \varepsilon x/2$ — the point the strike
sets (`Cell::kick`: $x = A$, $y = \varepsilon A/2$).

When an MPE performer glides a note, $\varepsilon$ changes every frame. Change
$\varepsilon \to \varepsilon'$ with the state left as it is and the state sits on
an orbit of a different amplitude: the *plain* retune (`retune_mode` 1,
`Cell::retune_plain`, "the printed justification, never the shipped sound").
What ships is a **re-base onto the orbit of the held amplitude**: with the new
$\varepsilon'$, read the invariant the state would have,
$q = x^2 + y^2 - \varepsilon' x y$, the $x$-amplitude it implies,
$a = \sqrt{q / (1 - \varepsilon'^2/4)}$, and scale the state by $s = A/a$:

$$(x, y) \;\mapsto\; \frac{A}{a}\,(x, y), \qquad \text{one rsqrt per retuned sample, none while } \varepsilon \text{ holds.}$$

Note what this is and is not. It is a homothety, $\det = s^2$ — not itself a
symplectic map, and the engine's class table says so in its own words:
"symplectic (a rescale to the same orbit)". What makes it legitimate is the
discipline of the *projected* row of the DSP class table: it measures a no-op
whenever $\varepsilon$ holds ($s = 1$ exactly) and its work during a glide is
bounded and gated. The suite's glide gate, −48 to +48 semitones over 2 s
through the ABI (step 86's run): re-based **0.097 dB** of amplitude ripple
(the bound 0.5); the plain recurrence under the per-sample ramp 0.057 dB and
stepped once per block 0.062 dB, both printed, not gated. (The cells chapter
quotes 0.63 dB for a one-block jump from the first landing; the suite's
current printout is the number to teach from, and the two should be
reconciled.) Problem 1.3 compares this rescale with the shear-scale
alternative that keeps $x$ and moves $y$ onto the new ellipse.

### 3. Non-canonical closed 2-forms: the Boris–leapfrog magnetic gyroscope (`voice_kind` 11, §13.13)

Must a symplectic 2-form be the canonical
$\omega_0 = dp_1 \wedge dx_1 + dp_2 \wedge dx_2$? Take a charged 2D oscillator in
a perpendicular magnetic field $B$. The Lorentz force is encoded not by
changing $H = \tfrac12\lvert p\rvert^2 + V(x)$ but by **twisting the 2-form**:

$$\omega_B = dp_1 \wedge dx_1 + dp_2 \wedge dx_2 + B(x_1, x_2)\, dx_1 \wedge dx_2 .$$

On $\mathbb{R}^4$,
$d(B\, dx_1 \wedge dx_2) = \partial_{x_1}B\, dx_1 \wedge dx_1 \wedge dx_2 + \partial_{x_2}B\, dx_2 \wedge dx_1 \wedge dx_2 = 0$,
so $\omega_B$ is closed for any smooth $B(x_1, x_2)$. (Suzu's field is uniform
over the voice and varies only in time: `boris_b` in hertz, 0..50, the poly
pressure's — rest + (full − rest) · swirl.)

The scheme, `suzu::Gyro::step`, with $y = \dot x/\omega_0$ in the cell's units
so that a free orbit is a circle of radius $A$: the **half kick**
$y \mathrel{-}= (\varepsilon/2)\, x$; the **Cayley rotation of the velocity**
by $\theta_B$, written as three shears — $y_1 \mathrel{-}= t\, y_2$,
$y_2 \mathrel{+}= s\, y_1$, $y_1 \mathrel{-}= t\, y_2$ with $t = \tan(\theta_B/2)$,
$s = \sin\theta_B$, exactly the rotation matrix — the Lorentz force does no
work, so $\lvert y\rvert$ is left as it was; the **half kick** again; the
**drift** $x \mathrel{+}= \varepsilon\, y$. Every factor is a shear, so
$\det J = 1$ on the 4D state and the energy breathes with the kick's splitting
but does not drift: ten minutes at 96 kHz under a 20 Hz field, $H$ within
±0.031 dB, the minute means drifting 0.000000 dB. The reason for three shears
rather than a cos/sin matrix is a Module-1 lesson in itself: a matrix whose
$c^2 + s^2$ misses 1 by a unit in the last place scales the energy by that
*every step* — $e^{\pm 1.7}$ over ten minutes — where the shears round as a
walk, not a bias.

Both the spring and the field are prewarped, $\varepsilon = 2\sin(\omega_0 dt/2)$
and $\theta_B = \omega_B dt$ (the Boris angle $2\arctan(B_{num}dt/2)$ with
$B_{num} = (2/dt)\tan(\omega_B dt/2)$). The degenerate 2D oscillator then splits
into a chiral **Zeeman doublet**, one circular mode each way round the field:

$$\omega_\pm = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + \Big(\frac{\omega_B}{2}\Big)^2} \pm \frac{\omega_B}{2}.$$

On $z = x_1 + i x_2$, $w = y_1 + i y_2$ the step is complex-linear with
determinant $e^{i\theta}$ and the two angles $\theta/2 \pm \psi$,
$\cos\psi = \cos(\theta/2)(1 - \varepsilon^2/2)$: the doublet to
$(\omega_B dt/2)^2/6$, measured within 0.001 cent of the formula over five
notes and six fields; with the prewarp off (`boris_prewarp` 0, the lab's red)
the pair is 1.21 cents sharp at B6. Sending $(x_1, x_2)$ straight to $(L, R)$
makes it the engine's first stereo voice — a mono line without the field, a
precessing rosette under the finger's pressure — and the
[orbit trace](../../suzu/orbit-trace/) draws the same pair into the water as a
spirograph; the strobe's own clock is the cyclotron period.

**Beyond the cell.** The same discipline carries the voices Phase 14 added,
each in the class table with its red control: the Toda ring's leapfrog in
double with the minute-mean $H$ drifting 0.000017 dB over ten lossless
minutes ([Toda ring](../../suzu/toda-ring/)); the billiard's exact rotation and
specular reflection, $\det J = +1$ on the 4D state to $2\cdot10^{-12}$ dB over
66 000 collisions ([Billiard](../../suzu/billiard/)); the Dirac network's Cayley
step $M = (I - \tfrac{h}{2}A)^{-1}(I + \tfrac{h}{2}A)$, lossless over a random
skew $J$ to $2\cdot10^{-16}$ ([Dirac network](../../suzu/dirac-network/)). Module 2
takes them up.
