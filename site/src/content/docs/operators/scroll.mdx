The two piano-roll layouts are timelines: every note is born on a fixed
*now-line* and the whole sheet drifts away from it like a tray on a conveyor.
The drift is itself a closed-form deformation — a uniform translation:

$$
P_{src} = P - \hat v\,s\,\Delta t,\qquad
s = \frac{\text{bpm}}{60}\times\text{roll\_speed}\ \ \text{canvas lengths per second}.
$$

`roll_speed` is canvas-lengths-per-beat, **default 1/16 = 0.0625**: the full
canvas holds 16 beats of history — four bars of 4/4 — so at 120 BPM ink lives
on screen for eight seconds. A value of 0.25 would flush the whole canvas
every bar, which reads as a waterfall rather than a drifting tray.

## Invariants

* **Exact.** A translation has Jacobian 1; nothing folds, nothing is
  sub-stepped.
* **Ingress is an explicit shader branch.** When $P_{src}$ falls outside
  $[0, 1]$ the fragment writes *fresh water*: ink 0, hue 0 and the **identity
  coordinates of its own texel**. This cannot be delegated to sampler state —
  clamp-to-edge would streak the boundary texel's old ink across the entering
  region, and clamp-to-border cannot work because fresh water is not a
  constant: the identity coordinates vary per texel.
* **Everything else rides on top.** Tines, vortices and feeds still apply
  after the scroll, so expressive gestures smear downstream like ink in a
  current.
* **Paper stays put.** The washi grain is screen-locked; only ink scrolls. The
  regression check: run a 120 BPM scroll over textured output and confirm the
  grain does not move while the ink does.

## Ownership

* The **horizontal roll** (pitch → y, now-line at x = 0.12, field moves +x)
  and the **vertical roll** (pitch → x, now-line at y = 0.12 from the top,
  field moves down). Marble-only layouts — "play the now-line" is deferred.
* **BPM is host-supplied** (a param the host may update live from a DAW or
  link source); the core never guesses tempo from MIDI. The desktop's lab
  bench nudges it with `B` / `Shift-B` for syncing against a metronome.
