A vortex at $V$ rotates every point around the centre by an angle that
depends on its radius, $\theta(r)$; the inverse lookup rotates by $-\theta(r)$.
Because a rotation moves every point on a circle to another point on the same
circle, the map is exactly area-preserving and can never fold, however large
the angle. midi-sink ships two profiles.

## Exponential (Jaffer)

$$
\theta(r) = A\,e^{-r/R}
$$

Shear concentrates near the centre and fades smoothly: diffuse, breath-like
agitation. The default for the mod wheel, the Airwave dimensions and wind
routing — "wind over the water".

## Rankine (rigid core)

$$
\theta(r) = \begin{cases} \omega & r < R \\[4pt] \omega\,\dfrac{R^2}{r^2} & r \ge R \end{cases}
$$

The core rotates **rigidly** — zero interior shear, so whatever pattern sits
inside spins as a solid disk and survives intact — while all the shear lands
in the crease ring at $r = R$ (the kink is intentional) and the $1/r^2$
fall-off outside. Mechanical, tight: the gesture literally grabs a disk of
water and turns it.

## Invariants

* **Exact at any angle.** A finite rotation never folds, so neither profile is
  ever sub-stepped. Two passes of $A/2$ equal one pass of $A$.
* **Area-preserving** by construction.
* Rankine's interior is *exactly* zero-shear: a ring cluster inside $R$ comes
  out bitwise as it went in, rotated.

## Ownership

* **CC 1 (mod wheel), Airwave CC 20 (Raise)** → global vortex strength,
  exponential profile, through the CC map; CC 21/22 drift its centre.
* **Two-finger twist** (tablets, Marble mode) and **right-drag** (desktop) →
  Rankine, with $R$ = half the finger separation and $\omega$ = the twist
  delta; the settings' *Vortex profile* selects which profile the gesture uses.
* **Not** pen orientation. Every attempt to stir a vortex from stylus tilt or
  azimuth leaked a phantom vortex (absolute orientation is noisy and has no
  zero) and was removed — orientation only ever feeds the vibrato booster, as
  a decaying derivative.

## Lineage

The exponential profile is the vortex of *Mathematical Marbling* (Lu, Jaffer,
Jin, Zhao & Mao, 2012). Rankine's combined vortex is from his *Manual of
Applied Mechanics* (1858) — see the [citations](../../reference/citations/).
The soft, viscous sibling of both is the [Lamb–Oseen swirl](../swirl/).
