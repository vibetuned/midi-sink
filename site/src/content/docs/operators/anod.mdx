The engine was never fluid at heart; it is symplectic — a coordinate field
deformed by exact maps. Suminagashi was its first *medium*, not the engine
itself: the composite that reads the field as ink phase on washi. **Anod**
is the second reading of the same field. Switch between them in the
settings (*Medium*: Sumi, Anod) — [the medium guide](../../guide/medium/)
has the player's side; this page is the mathematics.

**Strain, not bands.** Every texel of the field stores where its material
came from. Finite-differencing those coordinates between neighbours gives
the Jacobian $J$ of the accumulated map, and for an area-preserving map with
singular values $\lambda$, $1/\lambda$,

$$
\|J\|_F^2 - 2 = \left(\lambda - \tfrac{1}{\lambda}\right)^2 = \sigma^2 ,
$$

zero for the identity and for pure rotation, positive wherever the sheet has
been *strained*. Charged material — the ink, re-read — glows by it,
$g = 1 - e^{-\sigma/s}$ over a base that makes a fresh strike visible as
charge, like ionized gas over near-black glass. The charge phase bands each
filament between the palette's core and halo; the aux channel drifts the hue
per event. *Glow scale* is $s$, the strain a texel needs to glow: smaller is
hotter, sooner.

**The glow.** The discharge blooms as a photograph of it would: the emission,
blurred over a few octaves (*Glow bloom*, *Glow reach*), is added back to
itself and the brightest filaments clip toward white — a wide cold halo, a
neon mid, a white-hot core. The glass under it is black (*Glass darkness*),
with a screen-locked speckle (*Phosphor grain*). Prints and exports bloom
the same.

**The far field is the grid.** Water carries no charge and never glows by
strain. It shows the field itself: the deformed grid, iso-lines of position
plus a gain times the displacement, one family per axis, drawn wherever the
water has moved by more than a texel and nowhere it rests. A drop's
displacement reaches far — it falls off as $a^2/2r$ — so its lines reach far
too: one family converges on the note, the other bulges round it, the
picture of a dipole's field, and a burst's lobes and a spark's jagged shears
draw their own. The pitch of the grid at rest is *Grid lines* (`anod_pitch`,
lines per canvas height; zero turns the grid off and leaves the water
glass). The picture was first seen by accident — a half-float staircase
beating against a large window drew the iso-lines of the source coordinates
— and then drawn on purpose, with the accident's gains kept because they
made the picture the author chose.

**Switching is live.** The field is medium-agnostic, so switching re-reads
the same deformation history: a marbled sheet becomes a discharge record and
back, bitwise. The dip stays a key away for a fresh sheet.

## The medium owns its bindings

Under Anod every MPE dimension left at *Medium default* follows the
medium's table; an explicit mode or a CC route overrides it, exactly as in
Sumi:

| Dimension | Sumi default | Anod default |
|---|---|---|
| strike (note-on) | the [drop](../drop/), radius ∝ √velocity | the [spark](../spark/) on its charge: the blast, a burst of order `burst_order` along the note's axis, the jagged shears |
| press (channel pressure) | the ink feed | [torsion](../torsion/) spent around the note, as rate |
| per-note bend | glide (a local tine) | the [Chladni stir](../chladni/) — distance the rate, sign the sense; a vibrato stirs back and forth and comes home |
| slide (CC 74) | hue | the spark's streamer frequency |
| swirl (poly pressure) | the Lamb–Oseen swirl | tunes the torsion's wavenumber from its rest upward (and the spark's when the slide does not own it) |
| mod wheel (CC 1) | the vortex | the [Chirikov](../chirikov/) throw |

The one-consumer rule holds inside the table as everywhere: a wavenumber the
bend owns, the slide owns or a CC drives is left to its owner.

## Palettes

Electric blue, plasma orange and phosphor green under the same three ids the
sumi palettes use; a user palette is read as a glow — the gradient sampled
by strain. [Palettes →](../palettes/)

## Invariants

* **The Sumi composite is untouched**: bitwise against the 1.0.0 print
  fixture with the Anod branch beside it.
* **A switch leaves the field bitwise**; the composite is a read.
* **The charge's glow tracks its strain**: on the harness the charged
  texels' luminance correlates with $0.22 + 0.78(1 - e^{-\sigma})$ read off
  the same field by the CPU.
* **Water at rest is glass**, at 512² and at 1920×1080, where the half-float
  coordinates round: the rounding draws no line, and a small drop's grid
  reaches as far as its displacement and no further; a grid pitch of 0
  leaves all water glass with the charge unchanged.
* **Not in 2.0:** a long-exposure buffer — strain accumulated over time
  rather than read from the field's present state — is carried with its
  open question (whether a discharge should remember); the glow is the
  field's, now.
* **The strike survives a lossy GPU.** Six velocity-100 strikes on the Tab's
  Adreno keep six charges, as on the desktop — the reason the charge is the
  classic spark's size and not a thread.
