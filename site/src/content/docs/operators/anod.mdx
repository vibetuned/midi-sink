The engine was never fluid at heart; it is symplectic — a coordinate field
deformed by exact maps. Suminagashi was its first *medium*, not the engine
itself: the composite that reads the field as ink phase on washi. **Anod**
is the second reading of the same field. Switch between them in the
settings (*Medium*: Sumi, Anod) — [the medium guide](../../guide/medium/)
has the player's side; this page is the mathematics.

**Strain, not bands.** Every texel of the field stores where its material
came from. Finite-differencing those coordinates between neighbours gives
the Jacobian $J$ of the accumulated map, and for an area-preserving map with
singular values $\lambda$, $1/\lambda$,

$$
\|J\|_F^2 - 2 = \left(\lambda - \tfrac{1}{\lambda}\right)^2 = \sigma^2 ,
$$

zero for the identity and for pure rotation, positive wherever the sheet has
been *strained*. Charged material — the ink, re-read — glows by it,
$g = 1 - e^{-\sigma/s}$ over a base that makes a fresh strike visible as
charge, like ionized gas over near-black glass. The charge phase bands each
filament between the palette's core and halo; the aux channel drifts the hue
per event. *Glow scale* is $s$, the strain a texel needs to glow: smaller is
hotter, sooner.

**The glow.** The discharge blooms as a photograph of it would: the emission,
blurred over a few octaves (*Glow bloom*, *Glow reach*), is added back to
itself and the brightest filaments clip toward white — a wide cold halo, a
neon mid, a white-hot core. The glass under it is black (*Glass darkness*),
with a screen-locked speckle (*Phosphor grain*). Prints and exports bloom
the same.

**The far field is the grid.** Water carries no charge and never glows by
strain. It shows the field itself: the deformed grid, iso-lines of position
plus a gain times the displacement, one family per axis, drawn wherever the
water has moved by more than a texel and nowhere it rests. A drop's
displacement reaches far — it falls off as $a^2/2r$ — so its lines reach far
too: one family converges on the note, the other bulges round it, the
picture of a dipole's field, and a burst's lobes and a spark's jagged shears
draw their own. The pitch of the grid at rest is *Grid lines* (`anod_pitch`,
lines per canvas height; zero turns the grid off and leaves the water
glass). The picture was first seen by accident — a half-float staircase
beating against a large window drew the iso-lines of the source coordinates
— and then drawn on purpose, with the accident's gains kept because they
made the picture the author chose.

**Switching is live.** The field is medium-agnostic, so switching re-reads
the same deformation history: a marbled sheet becomes a discharge record and
back, bitwise. The dip stays a key away for a fresh sheet.

## The spark reading (v3.0)

The plasma above reads $\sigma$ as a *density*: every stretched texel glows
in proportion, a sheared region glows uniformly across its area, soft
edges, bloom. A spark is a different reading of the same field — a thin
core, a corona, afterglow, branches — and *Glow reading* (`anod_glow_mode`)
switches between the two. The reading is a reader: the field is bitwise
under it whatever its knobs say, and the plasma is bitwise 2.0's. Since
step 76b the *strike* follows the reading too: a plasma strike is 2.0's
blob episode, a spark strike is that episode with the
[leader stroke](../leader/) struck beside it.

**Ridges, not sheets.** A texel lights only where $\sigma$ is a local
maximum across the strain axis — non-maximum suppression along
$\hat e_{\psi\perp}$, with $\psi$ the principal axis of $J^\top J$ that the
[Kessho](../minerals/) stencil already computes (one function, two
readers). The survivors are the finite-time stretching ridges of the
performance: the Lagrangian coherent structures of its flow, in the sense
of Haller, the ridges of a finite-time Lyapunov field in the sense of
Shadden, Lekien and Marsden — which is why FTLE plots of chaotic flows look
like lightning tangles: they are this object. On the played canvas the
filaments come of their own accord — repeated stretching and folding makes
them, a swirl over charge draws it into threads the reader thins to two
texels. Isolated strikes never will, and the next paragraph says why. A
steep transfer, $1 - e^{-(\sigma/\sigma_{ref})^4}$, keeps the lit fraction
to a percent of the canvas.

**The plateau — why a strike needed an operator of its own.** The Anod
strike's shear episode is a stack of triangle waves at $k$, $2k$, $4k$ with
halving weights (the [spark](../spark/) page). The slope of such a stack is
piecewise constant, so the strain it leaves across the blob is a set of
*terraces*: flat levels, every texel a local maximum of its level, and
non-maximum suppression — built to thin a peaked profile — takes only the
lower terraces' edges. Measured: a blob episode under the reading keeps
about half its lit texels with the suppression on and every one of them
lies within two texels of the plasma silhouette; raw $\sigma$ across the
blob reads (max − mean)/max ≈ 0.5, the terraces' levels, not a ridge. On
the torsion scene, where differential rotation grades $\sigma$, the same
reader thins the rings to under two texels and the suppression switched off
reads twice that. The first build of this reading was viewed with the
strikes unchanged under it, and a branch trick — a threshold against the
phosphor grain — was built, measured and retired: it cannot make an endpoint
on a ridge it did not have. *A reader can thin a ridge and cannot invent
one; line-like looks need line-like operators.* The line is written by the
[leader stroke](../leader/), which charges the channel it walks, so the
ridge the reading lights is the stroke's own.

**The leader and the afterglow.** Behind the composite sits the *strain
buffer* (the long-exposure question of 2.0, answered): once a frame the
field is read through the stencil into a side buffer that keeps, per
texel, the strain being *added* summed over fifty milliseconds and the
same summed over *Spark afterglow* seconds, decaying. The first is the
leader — bright, toward white-blue, the only thing the bloom sees; the
second the hot channel cooling in the charge's own colour, at four tenths
of the leader's weight so the tip stands three times above its wake. A
discharge is an event: once nothing strains a texel any more, its leader is
gone within a few frames and its channel fades over the afterglow's time
constant. The field itself is untouched — a side buffer, never the
payload — so every fixture holds.

**Flicker.** The ridge threshold is modulated in time by the phosphor
grain's value at the texel — the grain itself stays screen-locked as the
substrate invariant demands; only its use in time changes — so the channels
flicker without moving.

Ridges light on charge only, as the plasma does; the water keeps its grid,
and the stroke brings its own charge with it. Plasma is the default; the
spark reading with its stroke is yours to switch on, in the settings, a
preset or the INI.

## The medium owns its bindings

Under Anod every MPE dimension left at *Medium default* follows the
medium's table; an explicit mode or a CC route overrides it, exactly as in
Sumi:

| Dimension | Sumi default | Anod default |
|---|---|---|
| strike (note-on) | the [drop](../drop/), radius ∝ √velocity | the [spark](../spark/) on its charge: the blast, a burst of order `burst_order` along the note's axis, the jagged shears |
| press (channel pressure) | the ink feed | [torsion](../torsion/) spent around the note, as rate |
| per-note bend | glide (a local tine) | the [Chladni stir](../chladni/) — distance the rate, sign the sense; a vibrato stirs back and forth and comes home |
| slide (CC 74) | hue | the spark's streamer frequency |
| swirl (poly pressure) | the Lamb–Oseen swirl | tunes the torsion's wavenumber from its rest upward (and the spark's when the slide does not own it) |
| mod wheel (CC 1) | the vortex | the [Chirikov](../chirikov/) throw |

The one-consumer rule holds inside the table as everywhere: a wavenumber the
bend owns, the slide owns or a CC drives is left to its owner.

## Invariants

* **The Sumi composite is untouched**: bitwise against the 1.0.0 print
  fixture with the Anod branch beside it.
* **A switch leaves the field bitwise**; the composite is a read.
* **The charge's glow tracks its strain**: on the harness the charged
  texels' luminance correlates with $0.22 + 0.78(1 - e^{-\sigma})$ read off
  the same field by the CPU.
* **Water at rest is glass**, at 512² and at 1920×1080, where the half-float
  coordinates round: the rounding draws no line, and a small drop's grid
  reaches as far as its displacement and no further; a grid pitch of 0
  leaves all water glass with the charge unchanged.
* **The strain buffer is a side buffer** (v3.0): the field's payload and the
  fixtures are untouched; the plasma never reads it, the spark's leader and
  afterglow and Kessho's exposure do. 2.0's open question — whether a
  discharge should remember — is answered by the spark reading: it remembers
  for *Spark afterglow* seconds, as a hot channel cooling.
* **Run to run, bitwise** (v3.0): one gesture script twice from one draw
  state (`sumi_set_seed`) yields the same Anod field byte for byte — the
  strike's phase was the engine's one draw, and the gesture clocks it rebases
  were the rest; a recording carries the state in its header.
* **The strike survives a lossy GPU.** Six velocity-100 strikes on the Tab's
  Adreno keep six charges, as on the desktop — the reason the charge is the
  classic spark's size and not a thread.
