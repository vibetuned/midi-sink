A drop of radius $r$ falls at centre $C$. Everything already on the water is
pushed outward so that the area it occupies is conserved: a ring at distance
$\rho$ from the centre moves to $\sqrt{\rho^2 + r^2}$. The inverse lookup the
shader evaluates, for points outside the new drop, is

$$
P_{src} = C + (P - C)\,\sqrt{1 - \frac{r^2}{\lVert P - C \rVert^2}}
\qquad \text{for } \lVert P - C \rVert \ge r ,
$$

and for $\lVert P - C \rVert < r$ the pass writes the **new ink phase** and the
local radial coordinate directly — the fresh ink, not a lookup.

## Invariants

* **Exact and area-preserving.** The map is a closed form; there is no step
  size and nothing to sub-step. The area displaced outward is exactly
  $\pi r^2$, which is what makes concentric drops read as rings of equal
  *area*, thinner as they go out — the suminagashi look.
* **Strike → radius ∝ √velocity.** Perceptual scaling: ink *area* tracks
  velocity, so a doubled velocity doubles the splash, not the diameter.
* **Ink phase in parity form.** The texel stores
  $\text{ink} = 1 + (\text{counter} \bmod 2) + \text{radial} \in [1, 3)$,
  water = 0. Any two field values interpolate across at most one band
  threshold, so half-float filtering never speckles a seam however many drops
  fall. The hue selector rides in the fourth channel from the drop counter,
  which re-bases on every paper dip.

## The crystal drop — habits (v3.0, libsumi 1.7.0)

Write the same map in Hamilton's action-angle coordinates about the centre,
$\rho = r^2/2$ and $\theta$ (so that $dx \wedge dy = d\rho \wedge d\theta$):
the drop is the translation $\rho_{src} = \rho - r^2/2$, $\theta_{src} = \theta$.
Now let the radius depend on the angle — any profile $R(\theta) > 0$:

$$
\rho_{src} = \rho - \tfrac12 R(\theta)^2, \qquad \theta_{src} = \theta .
$$

This is a *kick generated by a function of the angle alone*,
$V(\theta) = R(\theta)^2/2$, so its Jacobian is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -RR' \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
and $\det J = 1$ **exactly, for any shape, at any size** — no sub-stepping,
and each ray maps onto itself monotonically ($r_{src} = \sqrt{r^2 - R^2}$
for $r \ge R$), so the map is bijective for every star-shaped habit. That is
the whole theorem; the shader evaluates $R(\theta)$ in place of $r$ and
nothing else changes. The habit is `drop_habit` in the params — **circle**
(the drop above, bitwise), the calcite cleavage **rhomb** (face angles
78.1° / 101.9°, the polar support form), the mica sheet's **hexagon**, and
the **rosette** of $m$ tabular blades, $R = r_0(1 + \beta\cos^8 m\theta)$ —
read at strike time in the note's own frame (the pitch axis), so a held note
keeps the habit it was struck with.

Three consequences follow for free. The interior ink phase is assigned from
$r / R(\theta)$ rather than $r$, so **the rings inside a crystal drop are
homothetic copies of its habit** (gate: a rhomb's half-way ring is the rhomb
at half size within half a texel; the circle's, fitted with the rhomb,
misses by six). **Growth is the same map:** two habit drops of the same
shape at one centre compose to one at $\sqrt{r_1^2 + r_2^2}$, so the
pressure feed grows a rhomb as a rhomb — the boundary stays the habit within
a quarter texel over three hundred fed frames, the nominal radius growing
linearly in the pressure integral as it always has. And **the corners
crease**: where $R'$ jumps, $J$ jumps, and the bands outside the polygon
carry a line along each vertex ray — invisible in Sumi, drawn by Kessho as a
birefringence line that reads as cleavage. `habit_roundness` softens the
support form's maximum with a log-sum-exp and blends toward the circle (1 is
the circle; 0 the sharp polygon, allowed). See [the minerals](../minerals/).

## Ownership

* **Strike** (Note On) on every layout — the drop at the note's position, or
  at all three echo rows on Jankó.
* **Pressure feed** (channel pressure with `press_mode = 0`, the default):
  while pressure > 0 the voice's drop keeps growing. Because a centre expansion
  of radius $r$ moves an existing boundary $R$ only to $\sqrt{R^2 + r^2}$,
  fixed steps would visibly stall; the voice tracks its nominal boundary $R$
  and converts the wanted growth $\Delta R$ into the emitted pass radius
  $r = \sqrt{(R + \Delta R)^2 - R^2}$. Same operator, physically meaningful
  growth rate, deliberately unbounded — sustained pressure can flood the sheet,
  which is the Osmose behaviour.

  <Operator scene="feed" params={{ vel: 90, press: 90, frames: 150 }} caption="Same operator, two behaviours. Top-left: a note re-struck five times — separate drops, rings. Bottom-right: one note held under channel pressure — one band whose boundary keeps growing, no new ring." />

  The difference is the whole point of the feed: a re-struck note lays a new
  ring each time (new parity, new hue), while a *held* note grows the ring it
  already owns — the expansion is emitted at the voice's own centre with the
  boundary-growth radius, so the band widens instead of multiplying.
* **Breath** (wind mode) uses the same conversion but relaxes toward a
  breath-proportional *width*: a twenty-second legato line stays a line.
* **Marble mode:** a tap or a click. And, since v0.6, the **pressure
  gesture**: a long press (Shift + right drag with a mouse) lays a drop and
  then feeds it — hold or push up and the band widens by the same boundary
  growth; the pass is `sumi_add_drop` with the FEED layer, whose interior
  inherits the band already under the centre instead of laying a new ring.

## In the scene

`r` is the drop radius in canvas heights; `n` is how many drops fall on each
cluster in turn. In the feed scene `v` is the strike velocity (the initial
radius), `p` the channel-pressure value held, `t` how many frames it is held.
Every scene has `⏱`, the frames between steps — set it to 0 for the result
alone, raise it to watch the mathematics work.
