midi-sink never solves a fluid on a grid. Grid advection diffuses, and
diffusion turns crisp suminagashi rings into mud within seconds. Instead the
ink is an **area-preserving, continuous deformation of the sheet**, evaluated
by *inverse lookup*: for every output pixel $P$ the engine asks where that
water came from, $P_{src} = f^{-1}(P)$, and reads the ink there. Two
half-float textures hold the accumulated pre-image coordinates $(u, v)$ plus
the ink phase and a per-drop hue selector; each gesture is one more pass that
reads one texture and writes the other. This is Aubrey Jaffer's *mathematical
marbling* (Lu, Jaffer, Jin, Zhao & Mao, 2012), extended with two of his own
later closed-form fields and a few of ours — see the
[citations](../reference/citations/).

Every operator page follows the same shape:

* **The formula**, exactly as the shader evaluates it, in aspect-corrected
  normalized coordinates (non-square canvases are corrected before the
  mathematics, or rings become ellipses; distances are in canvas heights).
* **Invariants** — why it preserves area, whether it is *exact* at any
  magnitude or must be **sub-stepped** to stay away from a fold, and what
  composes with what.
* **Ownership** — which MIDI dimension or gesture drives it, and the
  *one-consumer rule*: a MIDI dimension has exactly one consumer at a time
  (`bend_mode`, `press_mode`, `slide_mode` choose it) so two operators never
  fight over one stream.
* **A live demo** — the release wasm, the same engine bytes every platform
  ships, embedded through the scene API. The sliders are the formula's
  symbols. There is no second implementation anywhere on this site.

Two conventions run through every demo. The scenes are **paced** — the `⏱`
slider is the number of frames between steps, and the operators that compose
exactly are applied as a run of small passes, so you watch the transformation
happen rather than see only its result (set it to 0 for the result alone). And
every scene works on **two ring clusters**, top-left and bottom-right, so one
operator can show two orientations or two signs at once: a horizontal and a
vertical tine, two counter-rotating vortices, perpendicular fold axes, a drop
beside a pressure feed.

## The nine

| Operator | Kind | Exact? | Driven by |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Drop](drop/) — and the pressure feed | expansion | exact | strike, pressure feed, breath |
| [Tine](tine/) | shear | exact (Jacobian 1) | per-note bend (glide), master bend, wind migrate |
| [Vortex](vortex/) — exponential, Rankine | rotation | exact at any angle | mod wheel / Airwave CC (exponential), two-finger twist (Rankine) |
| [Wake](wake/) | potential-flow doublet | sub-stepped ≤ a/4 | every stylus stroke — never MIDI |
| [Pinch](pinch/) — saddle, crossed tines | Hamiltonian saddle | exact (det = 1) | CC 74 with `slide_mode = 1`, pen azimuth, two-finger pinch |
| [Ripple](ripple/) | sine shear | exact (Jacobian 1) | per-note bend with `bend_mode = 1`, CC 102/103 |
| [Swirl](swirl/) — Lamb–Oseen | viscous rotation | exact at any angle | poly pressure 0xA0, channel pressure with `press_mode = 1` |
| [Scroll](scroll/) | translation with ingress | exact | the piano-roll layouts' tempo |

## Two rules that hold everywhere

**Continuous feeds are deltas.** Pressure, breath, swirl and pinch arrive as
absolute controller values, but the operators consume *rates*: a pass per
frame whose magnitude is the smoothed value × Δt. Feeding an absolute value
integrates into runaway strain; a delta relaxes when the hand relaxes.

**Paper is screen-locked.** The washi fibre and grain are sampled in screen
space, never through the deformed field. Ink moves; the paper it sits on does
not. If fibres ever drift with a tine, that invariant has been broken.
