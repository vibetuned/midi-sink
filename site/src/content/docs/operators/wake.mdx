Draw a stylus through the bath and the water ahead of the tip bulges forward
while the water at the flanks streams backward around it. That is the
instantaneous flow around a rigid cylinder of radius $a$ moving through an
inviscid fluid — the potential-flow **doublet**. In the stroke frame, with
$x$ along the tip's per-pass motion $\vec d$ (magnitude $U\,\Delta t$):

$$
\text{for } r > a:\quad
x_{src} = x - d\,\frac{a^2 (x^2 - y^2)}{r^4},\qquad
y_{src} = y - d\,\frac{2 a^2 x y}{r^4}
$$

$$
\text{for } r \le a:\quad P_{src} = P - \vec d
$$

Inside the tip there is no fluid: the body moves rigidly with the pen. That
boundary condition removes the $r \to 0$ singularity exactly and matches the
outer field with zero seam on the motion axis.

## The viscous profile: an impulse in a Stokes layer

The doublet is the *inviscid* limit. Since v0.7 the settings offer a second
fluid for the same stroke: the paint layer as a 2-D **unsteady Stokes** flow,
and the stylus tip as an **impulsive point force** spread over the tip radius.
The velocity of an impulse $F\hat{x}\,\delta(\mathbf{x})\delta(t)$ follows from the
stream function $\psi = (F/\rho)\,y\,(1 - e^{-s})/(2\pi r^2)$ with
$s = r^2/4\nu\tau$; integrating it in time gives the displacement in closed
form. With the impulse as a Gaussian blob of radius $a$ (the tip) and the
momentum diffused to $\ell = \sqrt{a^2 + 4\nu t}$ by the end of the pass, in
the stroke frame:

$$
\chi(S) = \frac{1 - e^{-S}}{S},\qquad \Phi(S) = \chi(S) + E_1(S),\qquad
S_0 = \frac{r^2}{a^2},\quad S_1 = \frac{r^2}{\ell^2},\quad L = \ln\frac{\ell}{a}
$$

$$
d_x = \frac{d}{2L}\bigl[\Phi(S_1) - \Phi(S_0)\bigr] - \frac{d}{L}\,\frac{y^2}{r^2}\bigl[\chi(S_1) - \chi(S_0)\bigr],
\qquad
d_y = \frac{d}{L}\,\frac{xy}{r^2}\bigl[\chi(S_1) - \chi(S_0)\bigr]
$$

where $E_1$ is the exponential integral and the normalisation makes the tip
itself move by exactly $d$. The field is exactly divergence-free and
mirror-symmetric about the stroke, and its far field is a $1/r^2$ doublet
tail like the inviscid wake's. Unlike the doublet it is a *linearized*
displacement, area-preserving to first order per pass, so it is sub-stepped
at $d \le a/4$, the same budget as the doublet (measured: $|\nabla d| \le
0.25\,d/a$ for every $\ell/a \ge 1.5$). The setting **spread** is $\ell/a$:
small is a sharp, close stroke; large is soft and far-reaching.

## Invariants

* **Magnitude is not a parameter.** It *is* the tip displacement
  $d = U\,\Delta t$ — wake strength is pen speed, by physics.
* **Sub-stepping is required.** At exactly $d = a/2$ the inverse map's Jacobian
  reaches zero at the rear stagnation point ($1 - 2d/a$), so $a/2$ is the fold
  threshold itself, not a safe budget. The engine subdivides every stroke
  segment so each pass stays at or under $a/4$, counting the passes against the
  same per-frame budget as glide tines.
* **Orientation test.** Ink directly ahead of the tip must bulge *forward*, ink
  at the flanks stream *backward*. If it renders inside-out, the inverse-lookup
  sign is flipped — the earlier draft's $+$ contradicted its own zero-seam
  claim at the front pole, and the field was re-derived from the doublet
  potential $\varphi = -U a^2 x / r^2$ that satisfies the no-penetration
  boundary.
* **Tip radius from force:** $a = 0.006 + 0.030 \cdot \text{force}$ canvas
  heights — a harder press is a fatter tip and a wider wake.

## Ownership

* **Every stylus stroke segment**, in both Marble and Play mode, rides a wake
  through `sumi_add_wake`. Desktop: middle-drag (the scroll wheel sets $a$).
* **Never MIDI.** The wake is physical, not musical, and is deliberately absent
  from the generated stream: a DAW recording of a pencil performance replays
  the notes, bends, CC 74 and pressure exactly — and no wakes, because a DAW
  has no stylus in the water. This is one of the two documented exceptions to
  the loopback-conformance rule (the other is CC 74 under `slide_mode = 1`).

## Lineage

The closed-form short-stylus-stroke field is Aubrey Jaffer's: *Oseen Flow in
Paint Marbling* (arXiv:1702.02106, 2017) derives an exact velocity field for
slow, viscous (Oseen) flow past a stylus and turns it into a marbling
homeomorphism by iterating it — he notes the displacement itself has no
closed form. midi-sink converged on the same problem from the inviscid end —
the potential doublet with a rigid tip — before finding his paper. The
viscous profile above takes a third route to the same picture: the exact
time-integrated response of a Stokes layer to an impulse, which *is* closed
form for a force at rest, applied along the stroke one sub-step at a time.
The lineage note is the least we owe. See the [citations](../../reference/citations/).

## In the scene

`a` is the tip radius, `d` the total stroke length; the engine sub-steps the
stroke internally so the picture is the same however long the stroke.
