midi-sink models a tray of water with ink floating on it. Nothing is simulated
on a grid: each gesture is an exact, area-preserving transformation of the
whole sheet — Aubrey Jaffer's *mathematical marbling* — so rings stay crisp
after a thousand strokes. MIDI drives it: an MPE controller's strike, press,
glide and slide become drop, feed, comb and hue; a wind controller threads its
drops with wakes from note to note; a plain keyboard plays drops on the circle of fifths.
On an iPad or a Galaxy Tab the canvas itself is the instrument — a pitch
lattice under your fingers, a legato pencil, a trumpet's valves or a
trombone's slide, and a standard MPE stream out to your DAW: on the
[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/midi-sink/id6810793641) and on
[Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.vibetuned.midisink). The same field
reads two ways — ink on washi, or a discharge on glass — and the app sounds
what it draws: a sampled instrument, or **Suzu**, a synth whose oscillators
are area-preserving maps on phase space, so a note cannot drift.
