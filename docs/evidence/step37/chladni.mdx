Sprinkle sand on a plate and bow its edge: it gathers on the nodal lines of
the plate's standing wave, a lattice whose shape is the ratio of two
frequencies. The Chladni operator draws that lattice on the water with two
crossed shears:

$$
x_1 = x + a\cos(k_y\,y), \qquad y_1 = y + b\cos(k_x\,x_1),
$$

with $a = (A/k_y)\cos\omega t$ and $b = (B/k_x)\sin\omega t$ — a **kick-drift**
pair, the second shear evaluated at the **displaced** $x_1$. Each factor is a
shear, so the Jacobian determinant is exactly one at any amplitude: **class
exact**. The inverse lookup solves $y$ first, then $x$. The "simultaneous"
form, both shears from the undisplaced point, is *not* area-preserving
($\det J = 1 - a\,b\,k_x k_y \sin(k_y y)\sin(k_x x)$), and a headless test
keeps it that way — it can never be "simplified" into being.

**Harmony as geometry.** The lattice's wavenumbers take the ratio of the
interval between the two lowest sounding notes, in just intonation: a fifth
is $k_x : k_y = 3 : 2$ — three waves against two — a fourth $4 : 3$, a major
third $5 : 4$, an octave $2 : 1$. The ratio is recomputed on every note-on and
note-off and glides smoothly; with one note held it keeps the last chord.

## Two insertion points

* **Live** (default): the composite reads the ink through the lattice, and the
  quadrature $\cos\omega t$, $\sin\omega t$ makes it breathe — the nodal
  pattern rotates and pulses while the field itself is untouched. Nothing
  accumulates; a paper dip samples the un-shimmered field, exactly as it does
  for the [ripple](../ripple/).
* **Bake:** every frame applies the *change* of the two amplitudes since the
  last pass as one kick-drift into the field. Kick-drifts do not compose
  additively — crossed shears do not commute — so a breath that returns to
  where it started still leaves residue. That residue is the marbling, the
  same way the ripple's phase drift is.

## Invariants

* **Exact.** Measured on the desktop harness: one pass moves the interior
  pre-image by 22 texels; the pass and its exact inverse leave 0.18 texel,
  the resampler's floor. The inverse is `sumi_add_chladni` with a negative
  amplitude — the *order* of the shears reversed, not just their sign.
* **The ratio is in the field.** After baking a 3:2 lattice, the x-shear
  profile down the centre column is dominated by two waves, the y-shear along
  the centre row by three.
* **The ingress rule** (fresh water where a shear leaves the canvas) applies
  as to the ripple bake, so the operator cannot duplicate boundary ink.

## Ownership

* **`SUMI_CTL_CHLADNI_A` and `_B`** (the two amplitudes, unmapped in the core;
  the desktop maps CC 106 and 107), `chladni_bake` (live or bake),
  `chladni_k` (the base wavenumber: one wave per canvas height by default),
  and `chladni_ratio_p : q` to pin the ratio for scenes and the bench (0 : 0
  follows the notes). Under the Anod medium's binding table poly pressure
  will drive the amplitudes (step 42).
* **`sumi_add_chladni(a, b, k_x, k_y)`** as a gesture: one pass over the
  whole sheet; $a < 0$ applies the exact inverse of $(|a|, |b|)$.

## Lineage

Ernst Chladni's sand figures (1787) are the picture; the kick-drift
integrator — the shear-then-shear splitting whose ordering is exactly what
keeps it symplectic — is the mathematics that lets the picture be drawn on
water without a fold. Both shears are the ripple's family.
